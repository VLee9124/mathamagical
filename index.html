Validating Trajectory Data Attribution During Capability Acquisition

Abstract

Influence-function-based methods like MAGIC provide exact first-order scores for trajectory data attribution, yet only retraining indicates whether those scores accurately predict data removal. We evaluate MAGIC on 86M- and 152M-parameter GPT-2-style models learning multistep arithmetic from scratch on filtered web text and four synthetic math families. Full-run attribution at 86M suffers from numerical overflow, and even with metagradient clipping, scores correlate near chance with retraining (linear datamodeling score, LDS, 0.17–0.22). In contrast, short checkpoint windows yield stable, accurate predictions at both scales (LDS 0.86–0.99 across four 100-step windows at 152M), whereas a single-checkpoint gradient-dot baseline shows near-zero correlation. Rankings of individual training blocks predict retraining only within that single run and do not transfer across seeds or hyperparameters (ρ ≤ 0.09), whereas format-level patterns replicate across runs. Two data families, worked examples and primitive facts, occupy 2.1–2.6× their corpus share in both the helpful and harmful 5% score tails, reflecting strong bidirectional local sensitivity distinct from net benefit under multi-epoch family deletion. These results show that trajectory attribution is reliable within short, validated windows and yields family-level insights that generalize across runs.

Method

From a mixed corpus to a retraining check.

Five steps. The third is the one the results turn on: we attribute a short continuation from a saved checkpoint instead of the whole training run.

Q1Horizon

Can we attribute the entire training run?

→The whole run is too long to differentiate.

At 86M parameters the running metagradient grows by 0.4–0.5% with every step the reverse pass unrolls. Over 30,518 steps that compounding overflows. Clipping restores finite numbers, not predictive ones. Stopping the reverse pass after 100 steps gives both.

Q2Windows

Do short windows agree with retraining?

→Short windows validate before, during, and after the skill appears.

The main 152M model goes from 0 to 200 of 200 held-out problems within the first third of training. Four 100-step windows along that curve each get a grid of 48 retrains × 8 queries. Median per-query LDS is at least 0.86 in all four windows and at least 0.99 in three; the lowest is the window where accuracy first appears.
